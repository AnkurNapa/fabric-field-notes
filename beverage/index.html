Fabric on the brewhouse floor

I came to Fabric from beer, then whisky and wine. Most dashboards in this industry do not die because of the tool. They die because the model does not match how the plant actually runs. This is the roadmap seen from the floor: the gap in each trade, and the Fabric pattern that closes it.

Beer and maltingWhisky and spiritsWineFood, beverage and alcobev

Beer and malting

A brewery is a flow, from grain to mash to wort to fermenter to bright tank to keg. Dashboards that model it like a retail ledger lose the chain, and the brewers stop opening them.

Speak the units: Degrees Plato, hectolitres, EBC colour, IBU, apparent attenuation.

Whisky and spirits

Spirits are measured in litres of pure alcohol, mature for years and are taxed on what leaves bond. Mix bulk litres with pure alcohol, or lose a cask move, and both the yield and the stock valuation are wrong.

Speak the units: Litres of pure alcohol, ABV, cut points, angel's share, cask age.

Wine

Wine runs on vintage time and a lot chain from block to pick to tank to barrel to blend. Count tonnes of fruit against finished litres, or drop the chain, and no winemaker will trust the yield.

Speak the units: Brix, tonnes per hectare, YAN, volatile acidity, litres per tonne.

Food, beverage and alcobev

Across food and alcobev the hard questions are the same: where did this lot go, what duty is owed on it, and why did demand move. Most of the answers sit in systems that were never joined.

Speak the units: Lots and batches, cases, excise duty by state, depletions, shelf life.
