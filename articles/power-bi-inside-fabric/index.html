Where Power BI sits inside Fabric

Power BI is a Fabric workload, not a neighbour. Semantic models and reports are Fabric items in the same workspaces as lakehouses and warehouses, billed against the same capacity. What changes is licensing, refresh and how close the model sits to the data.

Short answer

Power BI did not get replaced by Fabric. It moved in. A semantic model and a report are Fabric items, listed in the same workspace as a lakehouse or a notebook, running on the same capacity and governed by the same workspace roles. Three things are worth knowing on day one:

  1. Data and model are now neighbours. A semantic model can read Delta tables in OneLake directly (Direct Lake) instead of copying them in on a schedule.
  2. Licensing is per user and per capacity at once. Building and sharing Power BI content still needs a Power BI Pro or Premium Per User licence, even on a Fabric capacity. Viewers on an F64 or larger capacity can get by with a free licence and the Viewer role. On anything smaller than F64, every viewer needs Pro, PPU or an individual trial.
  3. Premium features come with the capacity. A Fabric capacity workspace gets 48 scheduled refreshes a day, XMLA read and write and deployment pipelines. A Pro workspace gets eight refreshes a day and none of the rest.

The semantic model is the layer people skip in their heads. It is where "net sales" gets defined once: quantity times unit price, minus discount, excluding cancelled orders. Every report that reads it inherits that answer. At Harbour Goods the Monday sales meeting used to open with ten minutes on why the regional deck and the finance spreadsheet disagreed about last week. Both were right about their own definition of a sale. One measure in one model ends that argument.

A ten-minute tour

You need a workspace on a Fabric capacity or a trial, with at least one semantic model and report in it. If you have none yet, any small published report will do.

  1. Find the items. Open the workspace and look at the item list. Filter by type: semantic models and reports sit in the same list as lakehouses and notebooks, each with its own icon. There is no separate Power BI portal any more; the Power BI experience is one of the Fabric workloads you switch between.
  2. Check the workspace type. Open Workspace settings and find the licence section (the label may read License info or Workspace type). It shows whether this is a Pro, PPU, Fabric or trial workspace and which capacity it sits on. That one setting decides refresh limits and whether free users can view.
  3. Trace the lineage. Switch the workspace to its lineage view (the label may read Lineage view or View > Lineage). Follow the arrows from the lakehouse to the semantic model to the report. If the model's source is a lakehouse in the same tenant, you are looking at the whole Fabric picture in one screen.
  4. Open the model settings. On the semantic model select ... > Settings. Look at the refresh section. An Import model shows a schedule. A Direct Lake model shows Keep your Direct Lake data up to date instead, because it does not copy data in.
  5. See who can view. Open Manage access on the workspace. Viewers on a capacity smaller than F64 still need a paid licence each, whatever role you give them.

What people get wrong

  • "Fabric capacity means nobody needs Pro." Creators and sharers of Power BI content always need Pro or PPU. Only viewers on F64 or larger escape the per-user licence.
  • "PPU gives us Fabric." It does not. Premium Per User provides most Power BI Premium features but no Fabric capacity, so no lakehouses, warehouses or notebooks.
  • "An F2 is a cheap way to give the whole company reports." Below F64 every viewer still needs Pro, PPU or a trial. Do the user count before you pick the SKU.
  • "Reports refresh." Semantic models refresh. Reports query the model. When a number is stale, look at the model's refresh history, not the report.
  • "Power BI and the data engineers can live in separate worlds." They share one capacity. A heavy notebook and a heavy model refresh draw on the same capacity units, so a runaway job on one side slows the other.

On the brewhouse floor

At Winery A the harvest intake team weighs every grape delivery at the crush pad, the cellar tracks each tank of wine and finance prices the bottled stock. Before Fabric each team kept its own spreadsheet and the tonnes never quite matched. Put the intake, tank and sales tables in one lakehouse and one semantic model on top. Now "tonnes received this vintage" means the same thing in the cellar report and the board pack. The winemakers can view it for free if the capacity is F64 or larger; the person who builds the model still needs Pro.
