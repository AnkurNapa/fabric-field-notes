Colour, labels and accessibility

Colour should mean something, labels should say the finding and the page should work for someone who cannot see red from green or cannot see the screen at all. Apply all three to the Harbour Goods report.

11 Oct 2026 Fabric as of October 2026 Checked against Microsoft Learn Ankur Napa Power BI

Short answer

Use colour to mean something, not to decorate. One neutral colour for most data, one accent for what needs attention. Write titles that state the finding, show units and drop clutter such as unneeded gridlines and every data label. Then make the page accessible: text contrast of at least 4.5 to 1, never colour as the only signal and alt text on every visual that carries information.

Why it matters for your career

Roughly one man in twelve has some form of colour vision deficiency. Public sector and large company reports often have to meet accessibility standards. A report that fails them gets sent back. Knowing the checklist puts you ahead of most people building reports.

The idea

Colour. Grey for context, one strong colour for the thing to look at. If everything is bright, nothing stands out. Keep meanings fixed across the report: if Electronics is blue on one page, it is blue on every page.

Labels. A chart title is the one sentence most people read. Compare:

Weak titleStrong title
Gross margin % by category and monthElectronics margin fell to 29.6% in September
Net sales by cityKolkata leads; Mumbai sells half as much
SalesNet sales, INR, Jul to Sep 2026

State units once (INR, per cent) and round to what a reader needs. Nobody decides anything on the paisa.

Accessibility. Microsoft's checklist for Power BI reports, in short:

CheckWhat to do
ContrastText against background at least 4.5:1 (WCAG 2.1, criterion 1.4.3)
Colour aloneAdd text, icons or position; never only red versus green
Risky pairsAvoid green with red, brown, blue, grey or black; blue with purple or grey; light green with yellow
Alt textFill in for every visual that carries information (250 characters at most)
TooltipsNever the only place an important number lives; screen readers cannot read report page tooltips
Sort orderSet it on purpose; the accessible Show data table follows it

Try it in Fabric

Open rpt_harbour_sales in Edit mode.

  1. Accent the problem. Select the matrix. In Format visual > Cell elements, turn on Background color for Gross margin % and select fx to set rules. Add a rule: if value is less than 0.36, use a light orange background. Leave everything else white. The three Electronics cells light up (33.8, 32.8 and 29.6 per cent). In September the Kitchen cell does not, at 38.3 per cent.
  2. Do not rely on colour. Add a short text box under the matrix: "Shaded cells are below the 36% margin floor." Now the meaning survives a grey printout.
  3. Strong titles. Select the matrix, open Format visual > General > Title and set the text to "Electronics margin fell to 29.6% in September". Rename the bar chart title to "Kolkata leads; Mumbai sells half as much".
  4. Alt text. For each visual, Format visual > General > Alt text. For the bar chart: "Net sales by city for July to September 2026, sorted largest first." The screen reader already reads the title and visual type, so describe what to take away.
  5. Check contrast. Put the text and background colours through a checker such as WebAIM's contrast checker. Light grey text on white usually fails.
  6. Try high contrast. On the View menu select High contrast colors and pick a theme. If a chart becomes unreadable, it was relying on colour alone.

Check it worked: three matrix cells are shaded, all in the Electronics row. Mumbai's 57,693 against Kolkata's 112,569 is 51 per cent, so "half as much" is honest.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Rainbow by default. A different colour per bar on a single-measure chart adds nothing. One colour, with the accent only where it means something.
  • Red and green for good and bad. It fails for the most common colour vision deficiency. Use blue and orange, or add an arrow or a word.
  • Titles that repeat the axis. "Net sales by city" tells the reader what they can already see. Tell them what it shows.
  • Data labels on everything. If your eye goes to the labels before the bars, turn them off. Microsoft calls this the squint test.
  • Hidden meaning in tooltips. Keyboard and screen reader users may never see it.

On the brewhouse floor

At Brewery A the fermentation board showed tanks in green when on temperature and red when off. The cellar lead was red-green colour blind and had been reading tank status from the numbers for years without saying so. Swapping to blue for on target, orange for off and a written "+1.5 °C" beside FV-03 fixed it for him and made the board faster for everyone else as well. The fix took ten minutes. The old board had been up for three years.
