Read the Capacity Metrics app: find what used the CU

Install the Fabric Capacity Metrics app, learn the four visuals on the Compute page that matter and work through one day on a small capacity to find which item used the capacity units and whether anyone was actually throttled.

11 Oct 2026 Fabric as of October 2026 Checked against Microsoft Learn Level 1 Ankur Napa CapacityGovernance

Short answer

The Capacity Metrics app is a Power BI app that a capacity admin installs from AppSource. It shows 14 days of compute use per capacity, item and operation. Read it in this order: the matrix of items sorted by CU (s) tells you who used the capacity, the Throttling tab tells you whether anyone suffered and the Explore drill-through on one 30-second timepoint tells you exactly which operations filled it. The Utilization chart is the one people stare at and it misleads most often. Bars above 100 per cent are normal while the throttling charts stay below 100 per cent.

What you will build

An installed copy of the app and the habit of reading it. To practise the arithmetic before you have real trouble, you will also work through one day of operations on a pretend F8 capacity used by Harbour Goods, a fictional retailer:

WorkspaceItems
Sales Reportingsm_sales semantic model, queried by six report users, refreshed twice a day
Supply Chaindf_store_stock Dataflow Gen2, nb_load_orders notebook, pl_nightly pipeline, sm_stock semantic model
Financewh_finance warehouse

The day's operations are in read-capacity-metrics-app.csv: 47 synthetic rows with item, operation, interactive or background, start time, duration and CU seconds. It is shaped like the rows you see after drilling through in the app.

Before you start

  • Capacity admin rights on the capacity. Only a capacity admin can install the app. Afterwards you can share the report with others.
  • A Power BI Pro or Premium Per User licence, or a Power BI individual trial.
  • A workspace to hold the app that is not itself on the capacity you are watching. If the capacity is throttled, you still want the app to open. A Pro workspace is the usual answer.

Step 1: install the app

  1. In Fabric, switch to the Power BI experience and select Apps > Get apps.
  2. Search for Microsoft Fabric and select Microsoft Fabric Capacity Metrics, then Get it now.
  3. Complete the AppSource sign-in if asked, then select Install in the Install this Power BI app window.

Step 2: connect it to your data

  1. Open the app from Apps. When you see "You have to connect to your own data to view this report", select Connect.
  2. Set UTC_offset to your standard time offset. India is 5.5, the UK is 0. Get this wrong and every spike appears at the wrong hour.
  3. Leave the other parameters as they are unless the dialog for your app version asks for a region or capacity ID. The capacity ID is the GUID after /capacities/ in the capacity's admin page URL.
  4. Select Next. Choose OAuth2 as the authentication method and Organizational as the privacy level, then Sign in and continue.
  5. Pick your capacity from Capacity Name. The first load takes a few minutes. Refresh if the pages are empty.

The app refreshes itself at midnight by default. Do not edit its semantic model: Microsoft supports it only for the app's own reports.

Step 3: learn the Compute page

The Compute page holds four things worth knowing.

VisualWhat it tells youWatch out for
Cards: SKU, Average utilization %, Peak utilization %The size and how busy it was over the periodBoth percentages are against base CUs, ignoring autoscale
Multimetric ribbon chartCU, duration, operations and users per hour for the top itemsSelect a column to filter the page down to 30-second detail
Capacity utilization and throttling (tabs: Utilization, Throttling, Overages)Usage per timepoint, throttling state, carry-forward debtUnfiltered, columns show the peak every six minutes
Matrix by item and operationEach item's CU (s), duration and users over 14 daysHover an item to split its CU by operation, such as query versus refresh

Two ideas make the rest readable.

Interactive and background. Report queries are interactive. Refreshes, notebooks, pipelines and nearly all warehouse work are background. Interactive work is smoothed over at least five minutes and up to 64. Background work is smoothed over 24 hours, so a nightly job's cost shows up as a flat layer across the whole next day.

Throttling is about the future, not the present. Each 30-second timepoint has a fixed allowance. Smoothing borrows from future timepoints. The Throttling tab shows how much of the future is already spent:

Future capacity already usedStageWhat happens
Up to 10 minutesOverage protectionNothing
10 to 60 minutesInteractive delayNew interactive requests wait 20 seconds
60 minutes to 24 hoursInteractive rejectionNew interactive requests fail
Over 24 hoursBackground rejectionEverything new fails

Each throttling chart crosses 100 per cent when its stage begins. A value of 75 per cent leaves 15 minutes of headroom before that stage. Work already running is never stopped. Only new requests are delayed or refused.

Step 4: work the example day

An F8 has 8 capacity units. One 30-second timepoint therefore allows 8 x 30 = 240 CU seconds and one day allows 8 x 86,400 = 691,200 CU seconds. Open the CSV in a notebook or Excel and answer three questions.

import pandas as pd
ops = pd.read_csv("read-capacity-metrics-app.csv")
print(ops["cu_s"].sum(), ops.groupby("type")["cu_s"].sum())
print(ops.groupby("item_name")["cu_s"].sum().sort_values(ascending=False))

1. Who used the capacity? Sort by item, as the matrix does. The Dataflow Gen2 df_store_stock dominates. It ran twice, at 02:00 and 14:00. Each run took about 38 minutes.

2. What does it cost every timepoint? One run used 61,200 CU seconds. Smoothed over 2,880 timepoints, that is 21.25 CU seconds per timepoint, or 8.85 per cent of the F8 allowance for the next 24 hours. With both runs inside the same 24 hours, the dataflow alone holds about 17.7 per cent of the capacity all afternoon and evening, before anyone opens a report.

3. Did the 09:41 spike hurt anyone? At 09:41 user-04 ran one heavy query, 3,000 CU seconds in 48 seconds, while 22 other queries ran in the same hour. Smoothed over the minimum five minutes, that one query adds up to 300 CU seconds per timepoint, 125 per cent of the allowance, on top of the 59 or so from background work. The Utilization chart shows a tall red bar well above the CU % Limit line. The excess is roughly 119 CU seconds per timepoint for ten timepoints, about 1,190 CU seconds in all. The capacity has about 181 spare CU seconds per timepoint to pay that back, so it borrows around three and a half minutes of future capacity. That is well inside the ten minutes of overage protection, so the Throttling tab would stay under 100 per cent and the System events table would show no InteractiveDelay. Tall bar, no victim.

In the real app, select that spike, then Explore, to open the timepoint detail page. Its interactive and background tables list every operation that put CU into those 30 seconds, with the user, Timepoint CU (s) and % of base capacity. The background table shows operations that finished hours earlier, because their smoothed cost is still being paid.

Check it worked

From the sample CSV:

  • 47 operations, 177,023 CU seconds in total, 25.6 per cent of the F8's daily 691,200.
  • Background 170,600 CU seconds. Interactive 6,423.
  • By item: df_store_stock 124,200 (70.2 per cent), nb_load_orders 22,000 (12.4), sm_sales 20,267 (11.4), wh_finance 8,000 (4.5), pl_nightly 1,800 (1.0), sm_stock 756 (0.4).
  • All background work together adds about 59.2 CU seconds to every timepoint, roughly 24.7 per cent of the allowance as a steady floor.
  • Six distinct report users. The 09:00 hour carries 23 queries and 4,980 CU seconds.

The useful conclusion for Harbour Goods: report users are not the problem. Two dataflow runs are seven tenths of the day. Moving the 14:00 run to the night, or making it incremental, frees more room than any report tuning could.

Where this breaks

  • Reading utilisation as throttling. Bars above 100 per cent are borrowing, not failure. Act on the Throttling tab and the System events table.
  • The wrong UTC offset. Spikes land at the wrong hour and you blame the wrong schedule.
  • Installing the app on the capacity it watches. When that capacity throttles, the app throttles with it.
  • Cross-capacity chains. A report on one capacity that queries a model on another shows its cost on the model's capacity. Check both.
  • Fourteen days only. For month-on-month trends, export or snapshot what you need before it rolls off.
  • Autoscale Billing for Spark. Spark on that billing runs outside the capacity and appears separately, so it never shows in this smoothing arithmetic.

On the brewhouse floor

A brewery runs a nightly dataflow that pulls the day's brewhouse readings, tank temperatures and packaging counts into Fabric. The head brewer opens a dashboard at seven each morning. When the dashboard feels slow, the metrics app usually tells the same story as this example: the morning reports are cheap and a dataflow that reloads every reading since the brewery opened is the heavy item. Make that load incremental, so it only takes yesterday's readings. The flat background layer then drops for the whole day.
