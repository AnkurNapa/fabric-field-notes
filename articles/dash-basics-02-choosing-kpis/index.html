Choosing KPIs that matter

A KPI is a number someone acts on, with a definition everyone agrees and something to compare it with. Write four of them as DAX measures on the Harbour Goods model and see why sales up can still be bad news.

11 Oct 2026 Fabric as of October 2026 Checked against Microsoft Learn Ankur Napa Power BI

Short answer

A KPI (key performance indicator) is a number that tells a named person whether things are going well, so they can act. Three tests decide whether a number earns the title: it has one written definition, it has a comparison (last month, a target or another store) and somebody would do something different if it moved. Four to six KPIs is plenty for a page. If you have fifteen, you have a list of numbers.

Why it matters for your career

Most arguments about reports are arguments about definitions. "Sales" with or without discount, with or without returns. The analyst who writes the definition down and puts it in the model once is the one whose numbers stop being questioned.

The idea

Pair every volume number with a quality number. Volume tells you how much happened. Quality tells you whether it was worth it. For Harbour Goods:

KPIDefinitionTypeComparison
Net salesQuantity times unit price, less discountVolumePrevious month
Gross margin %(Net sales minus cost) divided by net salesQualityPrevious month and a 36 per cent floor
OrdersCount of order rowsVolumePrevious month
Average order valueNet sales divided by ordersQualityPrevious month

The sample data shows why the pairing matters:

MonthOrdersNet sales (INR)Gross margin %
July 202633168,659.7537.3
August 202632154,160.0537.4
September 202631183,991.4034.8

September looks like the best month: net sales up 19.4 per cent on August. Margin tells the other half. It fell 2.6 points, because discounts got deeper in September. A page with only net sales would have celebrated it.

Two terms you will see. A measure is a calculation stored in the semantic model that recalculates for whatever is on screen: one store, one month or everything. A calculated column is worked out once per row and stored. KPIs are almost always measures.

Try it in Fabric

Open sm_harbour_sales from lesson 1 and switch to Editing. Select the orders table, then New measure, once for each of these. DAX is the formula language of Power BI semantic models.

Net sales =
SUMX ( orders, orders[quantity] * orders[unit_price] * ( 1 - orders[discount] ) )
Cost =
SUMX ( orders, orders[quantity] * RELATED ( products[unit_cost] ) )
Gross margin % =
DIVIDE ( [Net sales] - [Cost], [Net sales] )
Orders =
COUNTROWS ( orders )
Average order value =
DIVIDE ( [Net sales], [Orders] )

SUMX works row by row and adds the results. RELATED fetches the unit cost from the products table through the relationship you made in lesson 1. DIVIDE returns blank instead of an error when the bottom number is zero. Format Gross margin % as Percentage with one decimal place and the money measures as whole numbers with a thousands separator.

Open rpt_harbour_sales, add a Card visual with Net sales and a Table visual with orders[order_month], Net sales and Gross margin %.

Check it worked: the card shows 506,811 (506,811.20 before rounding). Gross margin across the quarter is 36.4 per cent, orders 96 and average order value 5,279.28. The table should match the monthly figures above.

Common beginner mistakes

  • No comparison. "Net sales 183,991" means nothing alone. Against August it means growth; against margin it means a question.
  • Averaging percentages. The quarter's margin is 36.4 per cent from total cost over total sales. Averaging the three monthly margins gives 36.5. Small here; large when months differ in size.
  • Vanity numbers. Page views of a promotion, total products listed. If nobody would act on the number moving, it is not a KPI.
  • Discount as text. If Load to tables inferred discount as text, the multiplication fails. Check the column type in the lakehouse table preview.

On the brewhouse floor

A brewery that reports only hectolitres packaged is reporting the September of this lesson. Pair it with brewhouse efficiency: the sugar that reached the fermenter as a share of what the malt could have given. Volume can rise in a busy month while yield falls, because the mash was rushed and sugar was left in the grain. The volume number says the team worked hard. The quality number says the malt bill went up per hectolitre.
