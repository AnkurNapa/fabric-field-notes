Publishing: apps, sharing and getting feedback

The workspace is where you build; the app is what readers open. Publish the Harbour Goods report as a workspace app with an audience, check who can see it under which licence, then use usage metrics and a short conversation to find out whether it is any good.

11 Oct 2026 Fabric as of October 2026 Checked against Microsoft Learn Ankur Napa Power BIGovernance

Short answer

Do not give readers a role in your workspace. The workspace is where builders work, with half-finished pages and test copies. Readers get a workspace app: a packaged, read-only bundle of the finished reports with its own link and navigation. You can publish one app to several audiences, each seeing only the content meant for them. Once it is out, the job is not done: usage metrics show who opens it and a ten-minute conversation shows whether it helps.

Why it matters for your career

Most report problems after launch are access problems: the wrong people can see too much, or the right people cannot open it at all. Knowing apps, audiences and licences turns "it works on my machine" into "it works for the regional team". The second half, collecting feedback, is how a report earns its second version.

Licences in one line each. Publishing an app needs a Power BI Pro licence and the Member role or higher. Readers need Pro or Premium Per User, unless the workspace sits on a Fabric capacity of F64 or larger, where a free licence is enough.

Try it in Fabric

  1. In the workspace that holds rpt_harbour_sales, select Create app.
  2. Setup: name it Harbour Goods sales, add a one-line description ("Weekly sales, margin and regional performance for regional managers"), a theme colour and a contact. Select Next: Add content.
  3. Content: select Add content and pick rpt_harbour_sales. Leave out anything unfinished. Select Next: Add audience.
  4. Audience: rename the default audience to Regional managers and add a security group rather than individuals. If you want finance to see a different set of pages later, select New Audience; one app holds up to 25.
  5. Select Publish app and copy the link from the confirmation.
  6. When you change the report later, select Update app. Readers do not see changes until you do.

Get feedback

  1. After a week, from the workspace select ... on the report > View usage metrics report. You need Contributor or higher and a Pro or PPU licence. The report covers the last 30 days: views, unique viewers, which pages and how people got access.
  2. Book ten minutes with two readers. Ask three questions: what did you decide using this last week, what did you have to look up elsewhere and what do you never look at?
  3. Make one change per answer, then Update app.

Check it worked: open the app link in a private browser window signed in as a test user in the audience. They should see the report and nothing else from the workspace. Usage metrics may take a day to show the first visits.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Readers in the workspace. They see every draft and test copy. Contributors can delete your report. Use the app.
  • Sharing with individuals. People change jobs. Share with groups so access follows the role, not the name.
  • Forgetting the licence. On a capacity smaller than F64, a reader with a free licence cannot open the app. Check before launch day, not after.
  • Duplicating reports for each team. Two copies drift apart within a month. Use audiences in one app.
  • Treating usage as success. Many views can mean people are hunting for an answer they cannot find. Ask them.

On the brewhouse floor

Distillery A publishes one app with three audiences. The warehouse team sees casks filled and moved this week. Finance sees the stock value in litres of pure alcohol, bulk litres times strength, which is how the excise office counts it. The master blender sees cask ages and sample notes. One report set, three views. Nobody in the warehouse can open the duty figures by accident. The first feedback session found that the warehouse never used the age chart; they wanted the bay and rack number next to every cask.
