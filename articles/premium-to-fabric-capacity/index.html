Premium to Fabric capacity: planning the P SKU to F SKU move

A design guide for moving Power BI Premium P SKU workspaces to Fabric F SKUs before the agreement term ends. What the retirement timeline actually says, how to size, why same-region wins and the licence trap at F32.

11 Oct 2026 Fabric as of October 2026 Checked against Microsoft Learn Level 4 Ankur Napa CapacityPower BIGovernance

Short answer

Power BI Premium per-capacity SKUs (P1 to P5) are being retired. Microsoft no longer sells new P SKUs. Nothing converts automatically. As of October 2026, Microsoft Learn does not give a single calendar date for everyone: each P SKU subscription retires at the end of its current agreement term. Customers on an expiring Enterprise Agreement or Microsoft Cloud Agreement cannot renew or add P capacity. Customers on an active EA can keep renewing annually until that EA term ends. After the subscription ends, the timeline is fixed: days 1 to 30 run at full size, days 31 to 90 are throttled. From day 91 every operation is rejected. The data is kept but nobody can reach it. The safe sequence is always the same: buy the F SKU, reassign workspaces, validate, then cancel the P SKU. Map P1 to F64 for a like-for-like start and stay at F64 or above if free users view reports.

The decision

The retirement timeline, exactly as Learn states it.

PhaseTimingWhat happens
RetirementEnd of your current agreement termP SKU subscription ends and cannot be renewed
Grace periodDays 1 to 30 after the endCapacity runs at current size at no charge; you can still reassign
ThrottledDays 31 to 90Interactive operations delayed; export and recovery still possible
Full blockDay 91 onwardAll operations rejected; data retained but inaccessible

Not affected: Power BI Pro, Premium Per User, Embedded EM and A SKUs plus sovereign clouds (Fabric is not available there yet). The real deadline for any given client is a date on their contract, which is why the first question in this guide is about paperwork, not technology.

The sizing and region choices.

ChoiceOption AOption BDefault
SizeEquivalent F SKU (P1 = F64, P2 = F128, P3 = F256, P4 = F512, P5 = F1024)Right-size from 30 to 45 days of Capacity Metrics dataStart equivalent, right-size after a month
RegionSame region as the P SKUDifferent regionSame region
BillingPay as you go (per second, one-minute minimum)Reservation for a large discountPay as you go during migration, reserve once stable
ShapeOne F SKU like the old PProduction F64 plus a small paused-when-idle dev SKUSplit only if the measured load fits

Same-region reassignment of standard Power BI items is effectively zero downtime apart from refreshes in flight. A cross-region move deletes large storage format semantic models and every Fabric item in the workspace (lakehouses, warehouses, notebooks, pipelines). Those must be captured in Git, their data backed up and then restored into a separate workspace so a Git sync does not delete anything. Regional price differences are not a reason to move. Microsoft says so on the FAQ page. The rebuild cost agrees.

What behaves differently on F SKUs:

  • Autoscale does not exist. Use manual scale up and down in the Azure portal plus capacity surge protection.
  • Pause and resume are new and useful for dev capacities.
  • Free viewers need F64 or larger. On F2 to F32 every viewer needs Pro or PPU.
  • Power BI Report Server comes with a Fabric capacity reservation (the decision guide says F64 or higher) or with SQL Server Enterprise Edition plus Software Assurance. Check this before cancelling.
  • Embedded is included with every F SKU, so separate EM or A SKUs can go.

Reference design

Harbour Goods, the fictional retailer used across this site, runs one P1 with 48 workspaces. Its agreement term ends on a date in the contract; call it T.

T - 12 weeks  Inventory: every workspace on the P1, its items, storage
              format, refresh windows, gateways. Flag Fabric items made
              during an old trial.
T - 10 weeks  Register Microsoft.Fabric, check CU quota, buy F64 in the
              SAME region, add capacity admins, recreate P workload
              settings (semantic model memory limits etc).
T - 8 weeks   Pilot: one low-risk workspace with a report, a scheduled
              refresh and a gateway connection. Validate.
T - 6 weeks   Reassign in batches outside refresh windows
              (Admin portal > Capacity settings > Fabric capacity, or the
              semantic-link-labs Capacity Migration notebook).
T - 4 weeks   Watch Capacity Metrics for 24 to 48 hours per batch.
              Sustained 100% means undersized: scale up, do not wait.
T - 2 weeks   Report Server licence confirmed. Cancel the P1.
T             Agreement ends. Nothing left on it.

The two-week buffer before T is deliberate. The 30-day grace period is a safety net, not a migration window. Learn says that too.

Questions to ask the client

  1. What is the exact end date of the agreement term for each P SKU? Is it under an EA, an MCA or something else? Ask procurement, not IT.
  2. Is the EA itself active and for how long? That decides whether you have this year or several.
  3. How many people view reports without a Pro licence? If any, F64 is the floor.
  4. Does anyone use Power BI Report Server on premises? Its licence path changes.
  5. Did Autoscale fire in the last 90 days? The F SKU has no autoscale, so peaks need a plan.
  6. Are there Fabric items in Premium workspaces already, from a trial nobody remembers? They matter for any cross-region move.
  7. Is there a data residency rule that forces a different region? Only that, or real data gravity, justifies the cross-region rebuild.
  8. Do departments want their own capacity to pause and scale? Then several F SKUs, each tagged for chargeback.

Review checklist

  • Agreement end date per P SKU written in the plan, with the 30, 90 and 91-day dates computed from it.
  • F SKU bought and capacity admins added before any reassignment.
  • P SKU workload settings recreated on the F SKU.
  • Viewer licensing checked against the chosen size (F64 line).
  • Pilot workspace validated: reports load, refresh succeeds, gateway works.
  • Item counts after migration match the inventory.
  • Deployment pipelines, workspace apps and CI/CD jobs still publish.
  • Report Server licence confirmed before cancellation.
  • P SKU cancelled only after every workspace passed validation.

Anti-patterns

  • Cancel first, migrate later. Cancelling the P SKU before the F SKU exists puts every report on the grace clock. From day 91 nothing answers.
  • Sizing down to save money on day one. F32 is half the price of F64 until every viewer needs a Pro licence.
  • Chasing a cheaper region. The rebuild of large models and Fabric items costs more than the price gap. The gap can move, too.
  • Treating the grace period as the plan. Throttled reports from day 31 are how the CFO finds out.
  • Restoring Fabric items into the migrated workspace from Git. Git sync can remove anything not in the repository. Restore into a separate workspace.

On the brewhouse floor

A brewery group with one P1 shared across sites has the same deadline as a retailer, plus a calendar the retailer does not have. Brewery A runs its heaviest refreshes after the Monday morning brew-sheet upload and again at month end, when excise returns are reconciled. Schedule the reassignment batches away from both. The month-end excise model is the last workspace to move, because it is the report a tax office will eventually see. It is the only one where a stuck refresh has a legal consequence.
