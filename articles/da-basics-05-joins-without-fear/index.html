Data analysis basics, lesson 5 of 8

Joins without fear

Lesson 5 of Data analysis basics. A join lines up rows from two tables on a shared key. INNER JOIN keeps only matches, LEFT JOIN keeps everything on the left and shows gaps as NULL. Use both to get revenue by region and find a store that does not exist.

11 Oct 2026 Fabric as of October 2026 Checked against Microsoft Learn Ankur Napa Lakehouse

Short answer

A join puts two tables side by side by matching a column they share, called a key. The orders table only says ST-014; the stores table says ST-014 is Pune in the West region. Join them on store_id and every order line gains a city and a region. INNER JOIN keeps only rows that match on both sides. LEFT JOIN keeps every row from the first table and fills the gaps with NULL. Choosing between them is choosing what to do with the rows that do not match. That is where the interesting findings hide.

Why it matters for your career

Real business data is always split across tables. Interviewers test joins more than any other SQL topic because a wrong join gives a confident wrong number. Learn to check the row count before and after every join and you will avoid most of the damage.

The idea

The orders table is a fact table: one row per event, full of numbers. Products and stores are dimension tables: one row per thing, full of descriptions. Joins connect them.

JoinKeepsUse it to
INNER JOINrows that match in both tablesreport on clean, matched data
LEFT JOINevery row from the left table, matches or notfind what is missing or unknown

Here is the trap that makes people scared of joins. If the right-hand table has two rows for the same key, every matching order is copied twice and your revenue doubles. Before you join, check the key is unique in the dimension table. Here, product_id is unique in products and store_id is unique in stores, so nothing multiplies.

Try it in Fabric

Use the three tables in lh_harbour from lesson 1. Open the lakehouse, switch the ribbon dropdown to SQL analytics endpoint and select New SQL query. The short names o and s after each table are aliases, nicknames that save typing.

  1. Revenue by region with an inner join:
SELECT
    s.region,
    COUNT(*) AS order_lines,
    CAST(SUM(o.quantity * o.unit_price * (1 - o.discount)) AS decimal(12,2)) AS revenue_inr
FROM dbo.orders AS o
INNER JOIN dbo.stores AS s
    ON o.store_id = s.store_id
GROUP BY s.region
ORDER BY revenue_inr DESC;
  1. Find order lines whose store is unknown. Change INNER to LEFT and keep only the gaps:
SELECT o.order_id, o.order_date, o.store_id
FROM dbo.orders AS o
LEFT JOIN dbo.stores AS s
    ON o.store_id = s.store_id
WHERE s.store_id IS NULL;
  1. Turn it round. Which products have never sold?
SELECT p.product_id, p.name
FROM dbo.products AS p
LEFT JOIN dbo.orders AS o
    ON o.product_id = p.product_id
WHERE o.order_id IS NULL;
  1. Margin by category. Margin is revenue minus what the goods cost:
SELECT
    p.category,
    SUM(o.quantity) AS units,
    CAST(SUM(o.quantity * o.unit_price * (1 - o.discount)) AS decimal(12,2)) AS revenue_inr,
    SUM(o.quantity * p.unit_cost) AS cost_inr,
    CAST(SUM(o.quantity * o.unit_price * (1 - o.discount)) - SUM(o.quantity * p.unit_cost) AS decimal(12,2)) AS margin_inr
FROM dbo.orders AS o
INNER JOIN dbo.products AS p
    ON o.product_id = p.product_id
GROUP BY p.category
ORDER BY revenue_inr DESC;

Step 2 shows the missing one: HG-40058 on 2026-09-05 from store ST-099, the store you met in lesson 2. The inner join dropped it without a word. Common beginner mistakes

  • Never counting rows after a join. 60 in, 59 out means a row fell through. 60 in, 120 out means a key was duplicated. Check both.
  • Filtering the right table in WHERE after a LEFT JOIN. WHERE s.region = 'West' throws away the NULL rows, so your left join quietly becomes an inner join. Put such conditions in the ON clause if you want to keep the gaps.
  • Joining on names instead of ids. "Pune" and "Pune " are different strings. Join on the code.
  • Hiding orphans. ST-099 is a finding. Report it; do not let the inner join bury it.

On the brewhouse floor

A winery's tank log records each fill with a tank code and a lot code. Join it to the lot table on lot code and every tank gains a variety and a vintage. Run the left join the other way and you find a lot that was received at the weighbridge but never landed in any tank. On the floor that is usually a lot that got blended on the day and nobody wrote the new code down.
