Workspaces, roles and licences

The four workspace roles and what each can do, who needs a Power BI Pro licence, why F64 is the line where report viewers become free and the two break-even figures that tell you when to step up.

11 Oct 2026 Fabric as of October 2026 Checked against Microsoft Learn Level 0 Ankur Napa GovernanceSecurityCapacity

Short answer

A workspace is a folder for Fabric items with one access list. Everyone on that list holds one of four roles: Admin, Member, Contributor or Viewer. The role decides what they can change. The licence decides what they can do with Power BI content.

Two rules carry most of the weight. Anyone who creates or shares Power BI content outside My workspace needs Pro or Premium Per User (PPU). People who only view reports can do it on a free licence, but only when the workspace sits on an F64 or larger, a P SKU or a Fabric trial. They also need the Viewer role, an app or a direct share. Below F64, every viewer needs Pro too.

And the licence side:

A free licence is still enough to create lakehouses, notebooks and pipelines on any F or trial capacity. Pro costs USD 14.00 per user per month and PPU USD 24.00, paid yearly, at Microsoft's published marketing prices on 11 October 2026.

The break-even, worked for one company. Harbour Goods, a fictional retailer, has 8 analysts who build reports and 260 store managers who read them. Prices are Central India one-year reserved list, checked 11 October 2026:

  • On F32: USD 2,778.67 for the capacity plus 268 Pro licences at USD 14, which is USD 3,752. Total USD 6,530.67 a month.
  • On F64: USD 5,557.33 for the capacity plus 8 Pro licences, USD 112. Total USD 5,669.33 a month.

The bigger capacity is cheaper because the viewers stop needing licences. Two break-even figures come out of this. They answer different questions:

  • Incremental break-even, about 199 viewers. If you already need F32 for the workload, F64 costs USD 2,778.66 a month more. Divided by USD 14, that is about 199 Pro viewers. Above roughly 200 viewers, step up.
  • Standalone break-even, about 397 viewers in Central India, 357 in East US. If the only reason to buy a capacity is free viewing, F64 alone (USD 5,557.33 or USD 5,002.67 reserved) pays for itself against Pro for everyone at those counts.

These are judgement figures from list prices, not a Microsoft rule. Rerun them with your own region, discount and currency.

A ten-minute tour

  1. Open the access list. In any workspace select Manage access in the top bar. You see each person or group and their role. Groups are better than names: a user in several groups gets the highest role.
  2. Add someone as Viewer and look at what changes. Use + Add people or groups, pick Viewer and ask them what they can see. They can open reports and query the SQL analytics endpoint, but the lakehouse explorer does not show them files.
  3. Find the licence mode. Open Workspace settings. The licence section may read License info or License mode. It shows whether the workspace runs on Pro, PPU, trial or a Fabric capacity and which one.
  4. Check your own licence. Select your profile picture in the top right. The panel shows your licence, for example Free, Pro or PPU.
  5. Try item sharing instead of a role. On a single report or lakehouse, select Share. This grants access to one item rather than everything in the workspace. It is the right answer more often than people think.

What people get wrong

  • Thinking F64 makes everyone free. Only viewers. Give a free user Contributor or Member and they need Pro, even on F64.
  • Buying F32 for 300 viewers. All 300 need Pro. The licences cost more than the step to F64.
  • Expecting PPU to run Fabric items. PPU is a Power BI licence. It provisions no capacity, so no lakehouse, warehouse or notebook.
  • Expecting free viewing the second you scale. Scaling from below F64 to F64 is near instant, but Microsoft says the licence change for free viewers usually takes up to a day.
  • Handing out Contributor so someone can read one table. Contributor can delete items, is not filtered by semantic model row-level security and sees unmasked warehouse data. Share the item or use Viewer.

On the brewhouse floor

A distillery's cask report gets read by more people than anyone planned: the warehouse team, the blender, finance and the excise officer who wants a monthly view. Distillery A has three people building reports and around forty readers, so on an F8 everyone needs Pro and that is still far cheaper than F64. The blender wants Contributor so she can fix a measure herself. Give her Contributor in the development workspace and Viewer in production, so a quick fix never lands straight in the report the excise officer reads.
