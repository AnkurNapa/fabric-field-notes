Capacity and CU, plainly

What an F SKU is, what a capacity unit measures, what an F2, F8 and F64 cost at list price in East US and the Indian regions, how smoothing and throttling work and when overage billing kicks in.

11 Oct 2026 Fabric as of October 2026 Checked against Microsoft Learn Level 0 Ankur Napa CapacityGovernance

Short answer

A Fabric capacity is a pool of compute you rent by the second. Its size is counted in capacity units (CU): an F8 has 8, an F64 has 64. Every workload, from a Spark notebook to a Power BI report, converts its work into CU seconds and draws from that one pool. You pay for the size of the pool whether you use it or not, unless you pause it.

Fabric lets a job run faster than the pool allows, then spreads the cost over the following minutes or hours. That is smoothing. Throttling only starts when the pool has already spent more than ten minutes of its future. Overage billing, on by default for new F capacities, can pay that debt at three times the normal rate instead of slowing people down.

A one-year reservation saves 40.5% at list. Prices are set in USD and converted at London closing spot rates, so an INR invoice moves with the exchange rate. OneLake storage is billed separately per GB and does not use CUs.

Smoothing, in one example. Harbour Goods, a fictional retailer, runs its warehouse on an F8 in Central India. Every night a pipeline loads the day's orders and deliveries and uses 6 CU hours, which is 21,600 CU seconds. That is a background job, so Fabric spreads it over the next 24 hours: 21,600 divided by 2,880 timepoints is 7.5 CU seconds in each one. An F8 timepoint holds 240, so the nightly load costs about 3% of the capacity all day long. Interactive work, such as someone opening the sales report at 9 am, is smoothed over a shorter window of 5 to 64 minutes, so a burst of report users shows up quickly.

Throttling, in four stages. The trigger is how much future capacity you have already spent:

Future capacity already usedWhat happens
Up to 10 minutesNothing. This is overage protection.
10 to 60 minutesNew interactive work waits 20 seconds before it starts
60 minutes to 24 hoursNew interactive work is rejected. Background jobs still start.
More than 24 hoursEverything new is rejected

Jobs already running always finish. Debt burns down in quiet timepoints and throttling holds until it is paid off.

A ten-minute tour

  1. Find the capacity in Azure. In the Azure portal, search for Microsoft Fabric and open your capacity resource. The overview shows the SKU and region. Pause, Resume and resizing live here, for F SKUs only.
  2. Find it in Fabric. Open the admin portal and select Capacity settings. You see each capacity, its admins and the workspaces assigned to it.
  3. Open the Capacity Metrics app. If your admin has installed the Microsoft Fabric Capacity Metrics app, open its Compute page. Look at the Utilisation chart first, then the Throttling tab. Utilisation above 100% is normal. Throttling above 100% is the one that hurts.
  4. Read the price yourself. Open the Azure pricing calculator, pick your region and compare pay as you go with a one-year reservation for the same SKU.
  5. Check the overage setting. On the capacity, the setting may read Capacity overage or sit under the capacity's settings in Azure. Note whether it is on and what the threshold is.

What people get wrong

  • Reading utilisation as a crisis. Bars over the line on the utilisation chart mean bursting, which is the system working. Act on the throttling tab.
  • Pausing to save money in the middle of a throttle. Pausing bills all smoothed and carried-forward CUs at once. A heavily overloaded capacity hands you a lump sum.
  • Treating overage as a cap or a speed-up. Overage bills at three times the pay as you go rate (USD 0.60 per CU hour in Central India). It is on by default with a 25% rolling 24-hour threshold. The threshold is checked every five minutes, so it is soft. It adds no memory or speed. Reservations do not cover it. Microsoft suggests keeping the threshold under a third of the SKU's daily CU hours, the point where scaling up costs the same.
  • Reserving the peak. A reservation is a billing discount for one region. It does not shrink when you scale down. Reserve the floor you run all day, every day.
  • Assuming three years is cheaper. At list, the three-year meter is exactly three times the one-year price. Check your agreement.
  • Sizing from data volume. Gigabytes do not tell you how many people open reports at 9 am. Measure a few full days, because background smoothing needs 24 hours to show its real shape.

On the brewhouse floor

A brewery's busiest data hour is usually early morning, when the overnight fermentation readings land and the production meeting opens the same dashboard at once. The nightly load is background work and smooths across the day. The meeting is interactive and smooths over minutes, so ten people refreshing a heavy report at 8 am can tip a small capacity into the 20-second delay stage. Moving the load earlier does nothing for that. Making the report lighter, or starting the meeting from one shared screen, does more than buying the next SKU up.
