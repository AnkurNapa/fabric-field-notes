Dashboarding basics, lesson 6 of 8

Interactivity: slicers, drill-down and tooltips

Slicers filter the page, drill-down moves from region to city inside one visual and tooltips add context on hover. Add all three to the Harbour Goods report and learn when each one helps and when it hides information.

11 Oct 2026 Fabric as of October 2026 Checked against Microsoft Learn Ankur Napa Power BI

Short answer

Interactivity lets readers answer their follow-up questions without asking you. Three tools cover most of it. A slicer is an on-page filter, such as a month picker. Drill-down moves a visual from a summary level to the level beneath it, such as region to city. A tooltip is the box that appears when you hover over a data point. Use them to support the main answer, never to hide it: the page should make its point before anyone clicks.

Why it matters for your career

A static page answers one question. A well-built interactive page answers the next three. That difference is often what turns a report from "nice" into "the thing the regional team opens every Monday". It also comes up in almost every Power BI interview.

The idea

Cross-filtering is on by default. Selecting a bar or cell filters or highlights the other visuals on the page.

Try it in Fabric

Open rpt_harbour_sales in Edit mode.

A month slicer

  1. With nothing selected, select the Slicer visual in the Visualizations pane and add orders[order_month].
  2. In Format visual > Visual > Slicer settings > Options, set Style to Tile. Three months fit as buttons.
  3. Place it top right, where lesson 4 reserved space.
  4. Select 2026-09.

Check it worked: the card shows net sales 183,991 and gross margin 34.8 per cent with 31 orders. Clear the selection before you carry on.

Drill-down from region to city

  1. Select the city bar chart. In the Y-axis well, add stores[region] above stores[city], so the axis holds two levels.
  2. The chart now shows regions: South 182,190, West 123,494, East 112,569 and North 88,559.
  3. Turn on drill mode with the single down arrow in the visual header (the tooltip reads like Click to turn on drill down), then select the West bar.

Check it worked: West opens into Pune 65,801 and Mumbai 57,693. The up arrow takes you back. The double down arrow shows every city at once.

A tooltip with margin

  1. With the bar chart selected, drag Gross margin % into the Tooltips well.
  2. Hover over Pune.

Check it worked: the tooltip shows net sales 65,801 and gross margin 33.8 per cent, the lowest of the six cities. You can also make tooltips read as a sentence: Format visual > General > Tooltips > Options, turn on Sentence format only and write a template such as {Net sales} in net sales at {Gross margin %} margin.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Answers behind clicks. If Pune's low margin is the finding, put it in a title or a table, not only in a tooltip. Microsoft's accessibility checklist says the same: key information should not be reachable only through an interaction.
  • Slicer sprawl. Five slicers on one page means five things a reader must check before trusting a number. Two or three, in a consistent place.
  • Busy report page tooltips. A tooltip can be a whole report page of visuals. It is slower and rarely read. Guy in a Cube's advice holds: one dynamic sentence often beats a busy tooltip page.
  • No reset. Readers get lost after several clicks. Add a Clear all slicers button from the Buttons menu.
  • Leaving every interaction on. Select a visual, then use Edit interactions (the label may sit under Format) to stop a slicer filtering a visual that should always show the full picture.

On the brewhouse floor

At Winery A the sales manager wants case sales by region; the winemaker wants to know which blocks of vineyard the best-selling wines came from. One page serves both with a drill-down from wine style to individual wine and a tooltip showing the vintage and the vineyard block. The sales manager never clicks. The winemaker drills twice and finds that the bestselling white came from two rows planted in a frost pocket that cropped light that year.
